LogRocket comparison

Desk research on LogRocket against the same five questions, from its public documentation, pricing page and live CDN bundles, checked on 20 October 2026. Every claim links to its source. Where LogRocket's docs don't say, this page says so rather than guessing. Full notes with more sources are in research.md.

Who they are

LogRocket was founded in 2016 in Boston by two ex-Google engineers and has raised roughly $55M, most recently a $25M Series C in 2022 (Crunchbase). It describes itself as a "frontend monitoring and product analytics platform". Its heritage is bug reproduction for developers: session replay with the network log, console and Redux state attached. Funnels, path analysis, surveys and the Galileo AI features were layered on from 2024 (LogRocket blog). It claims 3,000+ customers (logrocket.com).

Pricing shape

LogRocket's public page lists Core, Pro and Enterprise tiers, "Starting at $176/ month" on an annual plan for 25,000 sessions, metered on sessions pooled across projects, with final price depending on "seats, retention length, and add-ons". Conditional recording (rule-based sampling) is a separate add-on at about $860/month billed annually. Issues such as rage and dead clicks are paid-plan only. Retention length is a priced variable rather than a published number (pricing page, conditional recording). Third-party contract data suggests 100K+ session deals land in the $3,000 to $8,000 per month range (Vendr).

Where LogRocket is stronger

Fairness matters in a public comparison. LogRocket's replay viewer attaches the network log, console and application state to the recording, which is better for reproducing a bug than Amplitude's replay viewer. Its conditional recording buffers up to 10 MB of activity before a trigger fires, so the recording includes what led up to the condition. If MVSI's primary need were engineering bug reproduction rather than onboarding analytics and tenant-wide privacy control, those are real advantages.

Twelve points to raise

  1. Real page weight: the 8 kB shim loads a 171 kB gzipped second stage. Compare like for like.
  2. The documented snippet is synchronous and must run before application code.
  3. Default capture is broad and masking is opt-in per option, including for network bodies.
  4. No dashboard-enforced masking policy; every redaction control lives in tenant code or HTML attributes.
  5. No retroactive redaction; deleted sessions still count against quota.
  6. Settings are per project with a pooled quota and no org-level config push.
  7. Roles cannot vary per project for one user; custom roles and audit logs are tier-gated.
  8. SAML SSO needs a support ticket.
  9. Thirteen ingest domain families to allow-list in each tenant's CSP.
  10. No field-level form analytics documented; several session filters are non-retroactive.
  11. Analytics ceilings: 2,000 properties per session, no range queries on traits, limited funnel group-by.
  12. Rule-based sampling is a paid add-on.
